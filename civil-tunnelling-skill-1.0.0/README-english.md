# Civil & Tunnelling Engineering Academic Writing Skill

This skill adapts the modular architecture of `Ryyyyyyy233/JF-Writing-Skill` for civil, geotechnical and tunnelling engineering. The Journal of Finance / financial-economics content is removed; the orchestrator + on-demand reference design is retained.

## Triggers

`CTE-skill`, `tunnelling polish`, `geotechnical translate`, `civil writing`, `隧道写作`, `岩土写作`.

## Modules

- `references/sentence-templates.md` — sentence functions and section-specific moves.
- `references/phrase-bank.md` — technical collocations, reporting verbs, hedging and transitions.
- `references/paragraph-patterns.md` — paragraph-level structures for Introduction, Methods, Results and Discussion.
- `references/meta-discourse.md` — interpretation control, scope boundaries and evidence qualification.
- `references/usage-guide.md` — polishing, translation and full-paper audit workflow.

## Research coverage

The skill supports tunnelling and underground construction, rock/soil mechanics, engineering geology, TBM/shield/EPB studies, laboratory and field investigations, numerical/computational mechanics (including FEM/FDM/DEM/MPM), constitutive modelling and engineering-focused data-driven research.

## Non-negotiable rules

- Preserve equations, variables, units, numerical results, citations, geological facts, machine settings, support parameters, test conditions and boundary/initial conditions.
- Distinguish measured inputs, derived quantities, model outputs, interpretations and human-entered labels.
- Do not strengthen correlation into causality or numerical agreement into physical validation.
- Keep engineering terminology consistent.
- Treat patterns as optional references rather than mandatory templates.
- Do not use detector-gaming rules or intentionally degrade prose to imitate human irregularity.

## Scope note

The upstream JF skill describes a corpus-derived journal style. This adaptation does not claim a comparable tunnelling-journal corpus. It is a discipline-oriented professional reference. Journal-specific calibration should still use current author guidelines and representative published papers from the actual target outlet.
