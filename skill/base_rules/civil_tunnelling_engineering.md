# Base Writing Rules: Civil / Geotechnical / Tunnelling Engineering

Version: 1.0
Scope: civil engineering, geotechnical engineering, tunnelling and underground
space, rock mechanics, engineering geology, TBM/shield/EPB research.

These are fallback rules. A reviewed target-journal corpus has higher priority.

## Core principles

- Technical correctness and traceability come first.
- Write from a specific engineering or scientific problem, not from generic
  statements about infrastructure importance.
- Distinguish observation, measurement, model output, interpretation and
  inference.
- Preserve units, sign conventions, coordinate systems, symbols and parameter
  definitions.
- State the physical or engineering mechanism when the manuscript supports it.
- Keep claims within the evidence provided by the experiment, field case,
  analytical model or numerical model.
- Do not invent geological conditions, machine parameters, support parameters,
  material properties, monitoring data, validation results or citations.
- Do not force a limitation paragraph when the manuscript or target-journal
  pattern does not call for one. State validity boundaries when technically
  relevant.

## Abstract

Typical sequence:

engineering/scientific problem
→ specific unresolved issue
→ method/data/model
→ central quantitative or mechanistic result
→ bounded engineering/scientific implication

Rules:
- Name the engineering object early: tunnel, excavation, TBM, lining, rock mass,
  soil, support system, fault/fracture zone, face stability, settlement, etc.
- Include quantitative information when it is already present in the manuscript.
- Avoid broad infrastructure or sustainability openings that delay the actual
  research problem.
- Do not add unsupported performance claims.

## Introduction

Preferred logic:

1. Define the engineering/scientific problem and why it matters in the studied
   context.
2. Identify the relevant physical, geological, construction or operational
   mechanism.
3. Review prior approaches by problem/mechanism/method.
4. Identify the unresolved gap or limitation that affects understanding,
   prediction, design, control or interpretation.
5. State the present approach.
6. State concrete contributions that correspond to actual work.
7. Give a roadmap only when it fits the target journal.

Avoid:
- a generic "urbanization/infrastructure is rapidly developing" opening;
- chronological literature catalogues;
- gap claims based only on "few studies";
- novelty claims without a specific technical distinction.

## Site, geology and engineering context

When relevant, present only information needed to interpret the study:

- tunnel geometry and depth/overburden;
- geological units and stratigraphy;
- rock/soil classification or key properties;
- groundwater and hydrogeological conditions;
- discontinuities, faults, weak zones or anisotropy;
- in-situ stress where relevant;
- excavation method and construction sequence;
- support/lining system;
- TBM/shield type and relevant machine parameters.

Do not overload the main text with project history that does not affect the
research question.

## Methods

The reader should be able to understand:
- what was measured, simulated or derived;
- the physical assumptions;
- the variables and units;
- how parameters were obtained;
- how results were validated or checked.

Separate measured/input quantities from derived labels or interpreted
categories.

## Results

Preferred structure:
result/observation
→ quantitative comparison
→ physical or engineering interpretation
→ relationship to the research question

Use figures and tables as evidence, not as substitutes for interpretation.
Distinguish correlation from mechanism.

## Discussion

Use the Discussion to:
- interpret mechanisms supported by evidence;
- compare with relevant prior studies when citations exist;
- explain why cases/scenarios differ;
- connect results to design, construction, prediction or risk interpretation;
- state validity boundaries where they matter.

Avoid broad recommendations that exceed the tested geological, operational or
model conditions.

## Conclusion

- Synthesize the principal findings, preferably in the same order as the
  research questions.
- Keep quantitative findings exact.
- Do not introduce new literature or new mechanisms.
- Add limitations/future work only when supported by the manuscript, requested
  by the user, or consistent with the target-journal corpus.

## Language

- Prefer standard civil/geotechnical/tunnelling terminology.
- Keep terminology consistent: do not alternate between near-synonyms if they
  denote different engineering quantities.
- Use active or passive voice according to corpus evidence and sentence
  function.
- Use causal verbs only where causality/mechanism is supported.
- Avoid inflated claims such as "excellent", "remarkable", "significant
  improvement" unless the manuscript defines and supports them.
- Do not ban phrases mechanically; corpus evidence and context decide wording.

## High-priority integrity checks

Before finalizing any revision, verify that the following are unchanged unless
the user explicitly authorizes a technical edit:

- numerical results;
- units;
- equation content;
- symbols and variable definitions;
- constitutive-model names;
- material and geological parameters;
- machine and support parameters;
- test conditions;
- monitoring locations/times;
- figure/table/citation identifiers;
- factual project descriptions.
