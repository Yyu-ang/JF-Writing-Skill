# Method Supplement: Geotechnical Experiments, Field Monitoring and Case Histories

Version: 1.0

Use this supplement for laboratory tests, model tests, field monitoring, site
investigation, construction records, TBM operational data and engineering case
histories.

## Experimental / field-data contract

The manuscript should make clear, where relevant:

- specimen/material/source;
- sampling or site-selection logic;
- dimensions and geometry;
- equipment and sensor type;
- measurement range/resolution when important;
- loading/excavation/operational procedure;
- sampling frequency and synchronization;
- calibration or baseline treatment;
- filtering and preprocessing;
- missing data and exclusion criteria;
- repeated tests or replicate count;
- uncertainty or dispersion measures;
- coordinate system and sign convention;
- unit system.

Do not invent missing instrument specifications.

## TBM / shield / EPB studies

Separate these categories when present:

1. geology / ground conditions;
2. machine configuration;
3. operator or control settings;
4. measured operational response;
5. derived performance indicators;
6. human-entered or interpreted labels.

Typical variables may include thrust, torque, penetration, advance rate,
rotation speed, chamber pressure, screw-conveyor variables, cutterhead
parameters and specific-energy/FPI-type indicators. Use only variables actually
present in the manuscript.

When auxiliary systems exist, preserve the manuscript's exact force/thrust
definitions and do not silently merge total, auxiliary and main-drive
quantities.

## Field case presentation

A good case-history narrative usually follows:

site/engineering condition
→ observation or event
→ measurement/data source
→ analysis
→ mechanism/interpretation
→ engineering implication

Avoid turning the section into a project chronology.

## Results and uncertainty

- Report variability when available.
- Distinguish raw measurements from processed or aggregated values.
- State sample/window definitions for depth-, ring-, time- or chainage-based
  aggregation.
- Preserve anomaly-handling rules.
- When labels such as rock class, strength class, warning level or geological
  category are human-entered or derived, identify them as such.

## Validation

Prefer direct validation against:
- independent measurements;
- repeated tests;
- another sensor/system;
- excavation observations;
- geological records;
- established benchmark data;

when these exist in the manuscript.
